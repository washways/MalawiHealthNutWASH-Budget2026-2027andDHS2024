How District WASH Risk Index Is Calculated

Step 1: Convert Service Indicators To Risk Components

For protective indicators, we invert the percentage so higher values represent higher WASH risk.

water_gap = 100 - basic_water_service_pct
sanitation_gap = 100 - basic_sanitation_service_pct
handwashing_gap = 100 - basic_handwashing_facility_pct
safe_water_gap = 100 - safely_managed_drinking_water_pct
open_defecation_risk = open_defecation_pct
e_coli_risk = e_coli_in_household_water_pct

Step 2: Build District WASH Risk Index

The WASH Risk Index is a weighted average of the six components above, on a 0-100 scale (defaults to equal weights).

wash_risk_index = mean(
  water_gap,
  sanitation_gap,
  handwashing_gap,
  safe_water_gap,
  open_defecation_risk,
  e_coli_risk
)

Step 3: Underfunding Signal

Districts are ranked by budget (largest = rank 1) and by WASH risk (highest risk = rank 1).

underfunding_signal = budget_rank - wash_risk_rank

positive  -> underfunding signal
negative  -> overfunding signal
near zero -> budget and risk ranks broadly aligned

The dashboard paints the gap as a signed number so districts with higher risk ranks than budgets surface for targeted advocacy.

Nutrition Risk Index

The Nutrition Risk Index is a weighted average of stunting, wasting, and underweight percentages. Higher values signal higher undernutrition burden.

nutrition_risk_index = mean(stunting_pct, wasting_pct, underweight_pct)

We also compute a blended index to compare WASH risk with nutrition risk.

wash_nutrition_blend_index = (0.7 * wash_risk_index) + (0.3 * nutrition_risk_index)

Sector budgets & subnational allocations

The sector tabs layer district allocation signals atop national ministry votes, so you can trace how central health, nutrition, and water budgets finance delivery.

The tabs highlight major cost categories (e.g., MOHS service delivery, MOAIWD construction, social cash transfers) and the staffing pressure signalled by personnel envelopes and water board operations.
