Methodology And Assumptions

Detailed explanation of derived scores, joins, and judgment choices

Source Base

This page combines extracted figures from the Annual Economic Report, Financial Statement, Programme Based Budget, and Public Sector Investment Programme. It mixes directly extracted line items with curated district, staffing, and project evidence where the source PDFs are fragmented or split across pages.

The result is an analytical reconstruction designed to support budget reading, not a full digital clone of the printed books.

Units

Programme and expenditure lines are treated as Malawi Kwacha in millions when drawn from programme-budget tables. CDF values are stored as raw Malawi Kwacha amounts from the annex and then reformatted for display.

Charts and tables therefore compare like with like within each panel, but should not be added mechanically across mixed-unit sections.

District Geometry

The CDF map uses dissolved and simplified district, city, and municipal geometry. The original ward file was collapsed to district-level shapes and reduced to the minimum join field, DistrictNa, before being embedded in this HTML file.

This was done to improve reliability and speed. The policy question in this page is district allocation pattern, so ward geometry added load without adding analytical value.

Name Normalisation

Map joins use a normalisation layer so district and council labels in the CDF annex match the embedded geometry. Examples include M'mbelwa District Council to Mzimba, Likoma Islands to Likoma, and known municipal spelling variants.

This is a reconciliation step to prevent false join failures, not a reclassification of local authorities.

WASH Overlay Formula

The WASH priority overlay is a derived score. The implemented formula is 25 + min(25, CDF total / 4,000,000,000) + district bonus, capped at 100.

The district bonus is only applied where the source documents name strong water or sanitation exposure, such as Lilongwe water and sanitation works, Blantyre sanitation counterpart financing, Mzuzu sewerage works, Rumphi water and sanitation, Karonga counterpart financing, or repeated waterboard-linked investments.

WASH Overlay Justification

The base term reflects the idea that larger district allocations raise the importance of local infrastructure conditions. The bonus term prevents the map from ignoring districts where named WASH investment or service pressure is clearly visible in the budget evidence.

This matters because the source documents repeatedly connect sanitation, water continuity, hygiene, and sewerage to health-facility readiness, school conditions, and urban child outcomes.

Social Vulnerability Formula

The Social vulnerability overlay is also a derived score. The implemented formula is 20 + min(20, CDF total / 5,000,000,000) + district bonus, capped at 100.

The district bonus is assigned where the evidence base points to stronger social-sector stress, such as mortality pressure, climate-shock exposure, urban caseload concentration, or repeated service-delivery strain.

Social Vulnerability Justification

CDF scale alone is not a vulnerability measure. The bonus term exists to distinguish districts with similar fiscal size but different child-facing social pressure. This keeps the map anchored to spending while still acknowledging unequal service stress across districts.

These overlay scores are therefore prioritisation aids, not official deprivation indices.

Charts And Rankings

The added CDF charts rank the highest-value districts under the current measure. They are top-N comparisons intended to surface the strongest signals quickly. They are not national averages or statistically weighted national indicators.

Where no district bonus is present, the map note falls back to a generic explanation so users can tell the score is mostly being driven by the base formula.

Interpretation Boundary

This page is meant for strategy, targeting discussion, and evidence-led advocacy. It should not be treated as an audited financial record or as a substitute for formal district vulnerability datasets.

Where stricter proof is required, the next step would be to replace bonus dictionaries with cited district indicators from a structured official dataset.
